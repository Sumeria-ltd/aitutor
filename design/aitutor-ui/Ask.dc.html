Why does the Clausius inequality become an equality for a reversible process?

Session 5 introduces it as ∮ δQ/T ≤ 0, and the ≤ is there because of entropy generated inside the system. Slide 12 gives the two things that generate it — a finite temperature difference across the boundary, and friction — and states that both vanish only when every step of the cycle is reversible. S5 · p.12

So the generated term drops out and the relation closes to an equality. Your session 4 notes come at the same point from the other direction: the Carnot cycle is used precisely because it is the case where the equality holds, which is what lets the efficiency bound be derived at all. S4 · p.7

PHYS 214 — LECTURE 5
The Clausius inequality
·For any cycle, ∮ δQ/T ≤ 0, with T the boundary temperature.
·The inequality is entropy generated inside the system: finite ΔT across the boundary, and friction.
·Both vanish only if every step is reversible. Then ∮ δQ/T = 0.
·This is what lets us define S as a state function in the next slide.
·Worked example: irreversible expansion, ΔT = 40 K at the wall.
