This lecture was about entropy. The idea is that in a closed system entropy never goes down, it either stays the same or increases, and that's what fixes the direction heat flows in — always hot to cold, never the other way round on its own.

Entropy is basically how disordered the system is, so a gas spreading out through a container has more of it than the same gas squeezed into one corner.

He also did an example with a hot block and a cold block touching, where the total entropy of the two together goes up even though the hot one's goes down. So you have to look at the whole system, not one piece of it.
