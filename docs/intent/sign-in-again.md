# Sign in again

## PROBLEM

By November a student has a term of work inside AITutor: forty sessions, their own summaries, practice results, a readiness picture assembled over three months. That accumulation is the whole reason to keep using it — and it is exactly what makes losing access catastrophic in a way it never was in week one.

The moments a student needs to get back in are the worst possible moments to be blocked. A new phone. Exam week, at two in the morning. A borrowed library computer because their own laptop is dead. A password chosen in September and never typed since, because their phone remembered it for them.

Being locked out here does not merely irritate. It destroys the thing the product spent a semester building. A student who cannot reach their own material the week they need it most does not come back next term.

## USER

A returning student — on a new device, after a long gap, or under exam-week time pressure.

## OUTCOME

The student gets back to their material reliably, from any device, without ceremony. Returning on the phone they always use is effectively instant and requires no thought. Returning on an unfamiliar device in a bad moment still takes a couple of minutes and needs nothing they left at home.

Recovery is a first-class path rather than an afterthought, because what is at stake behind it is a term of irreplaceable work that exists nowhere else.

## SUCCESS

- Students return across a whole term, not only in the fortnight after they signed up.
- Signing in on a familiar device almost never requires the student to stop and think.
- Students who begin account recovery complete it. An abandoned recovery is a lost student and a lost semester of their work in one event.
- Lock-outs do not cluster in exam weeks, when both the need and the cost of failing are at their highest.

## LIMITS

- No student may lose a term's work to a forgotten credential. Recovery must always exist, and must not depend on anything they cannot reach from a library computer.
- Staying signed in on a personal device is the normal case. Repeatedly re-authenticating a student on their own phone teaches them to stop opening it.
- Access must be reachable from a device the student does not own and will never use again.
- Only the account holder reaches the material. Recovery must not become a way in for anyone else — the privacy promise is only as strong as this path.
- Signing in must not stand between a student and the moment of capture any more than strictly necessary. Someone with a file and two spare minutes should not spend them here.

## NOT NOW

- Institutional single sign-on
- Device management, session listings, or remote sign-out
- Shared or delegated access to another student's account
- Mandatory two-factor authentication, or anything requiring a second physical device
