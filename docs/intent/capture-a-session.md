# Capture a session

## PROBLEM

The lecture ends. The student walks out holding two things: a file — emailed, downloaded, or photographed off the whiteboard — and a fresh but fragile understanding of what just happened. Both decay immediately, and neither decay is noticed.

The file goes to Downloads and joins two hundred others with names like `Lecture4_final_v2.pdf`. The understanding fades over the following days, and nothing ever asks the student to produce it, so they never find out it is going. Nothing in the student's life marks that a class happened, connects the artifact to what it was about, or asks what they took away while the answer is still available.

The whole semester is lost in this gap. Students intend to review, and do not. By the time either the file or the understanding is needed, one is unfindable and the other is gone.

## USER

A student who has just left a class — or is catching up on one they missed — holding material and a memory that is still warm.

## OUTCOME

Each class that happened has a marked place in its course, with its material attached and, when the student does the work, their own account of what it covered. The file stops being an anonymous download and becomes *session 5 of Thermodynamics, the one about entropy*.

Capturing takes a minute or two, works from a phone in the corridor outside the lecture hall, and does not require the student to have understood everything yet. The invitation to write down what they took away arrives here, while the memory is warm — not in exam week, when it is already gone.

## SUCCESS

- Sessions are captured within a day or two of the class, rather than in a panicked batch before exams.
- Sessions keep being added past week three — the point where good intentions reliably die and where this product lives or dies with them.
- A meaningful share of sessions carry the student's own words, not only an attached file. A course of pure attachments is a filing cabinet, and filing cabinets do not teach.
- A student can find a specific session's material months later without hunting for it.

## LIMITS

- Capture must work when the student has only a file and nothing yet to say about it. Writing their own account is always invited and never required, or capture stops happening at all.
- That account is the student's work. It may be prompted, questioned, or responded to. It must never be written for them and offered back as theirs.
- Sessions can be added late, out of order, or in a rush, without penalty and without nagging. Students fall behind; the product absorbs that rather than moralizing about it.
- Material must be capturable from a phone, immediately after class, on a bad connection.
- What the student attaches is theirs, and private to them.

## NOT NOW

- Recording or transcribing lecture audio
- Reminders, streaks, or any prompting to capture
- Pulling material automatically from a university system
- Live note-taking during the lecture itself
