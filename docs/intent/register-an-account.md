# Register an account

## PROBLEM

A student in the corridor after a lecture, holding a file they want to keep, meets a signup form. That is where most of them leave — not because registering is difficult, but because it arrives before the product has done anything for them. They came to solve a thirty-second problem and were asked to make a commitment first.

But the account is not bureaucracy, and treating it as a formality misses what it carries. Everything this product promises depends on it. Material has to survive a lost phone, a reinstalled app, a switch from laptop to phone mid-week. A term's accumulated work is only worth building if it is durable and it is genuinely theirs. And the promise that a course is private to one student means nothing without an account to draw that boundary.

So registration holds a real tension: it is the thing that makes the product's central promises true, and it is simultaneously the largest point of abandonment, sitting at the worst possible moment.

## USER

A student meeting AITutor for the first time — usually on a phone, usually with something specific they wanted to do right now.

## OUTCOME

The student ends up with an account that makes their work durable, portable and private, reached with the least possible interruption to whatever they actually came to do. They understand what the account is *for* — this is where your material lives, and only you can see it — rather than only that one was demanded of them.

Signing up should feel like securing something, not like paying a toll.

## SUCCESS

- Most students who begin registration finish it. Abandonment here is the cheapest failure to fix and the most expensive to leave alone.
- Students reach a first moment of value soon after registering. An account that stays empty belongs to a student who is not coming back.
- Students sign in from more than one device across a term. That is the proof that durability is real to them rather than merely claimed.
- Few students register twice. A duplicate account means the first one's material is stranded, and the student will not know why the product feels empty.

## LIMITS

- Registration must be completable on a phone, in a corridor, on a bad connection, in under a minute.
- The account is the privacy boundary. What that means — this material is yours alone — must be evident at the moment of signing up, not buried where nobody reads it.
- Collect only what is needed to make access durable and recoverable. Every additional field costs students.
- No institutional affiliation is required, and none is verified. The product must not imply a student has been vouched for by their university.
- A student must be able to leave, taking or deleting their material. Ownership that cannot be exercised is not ownership.

## NOT NOW

- University or institutional single sign-on
- Verifying that a student genuinely attends a given university
- Paid plans, trials, or anything requiring payment details
- Invitations, referrals, or any social connection between students
- Any account type other than a single student
