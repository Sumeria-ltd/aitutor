# Set up a course

## PROBLEM

A student is three weeks into a semester carrying five courses, and none of them has a home. Slides sit in Downloads, notes are in a paper notebook, the syllabus PDF was opened once and never again. Nothing in the student's life holds the *shape* of a course — what it is supposed to teach them, and where they stand against that. So "am I behind in this course?" is a feeling they carry, never a fact they can check.

The information that would make it a fact already exists. Universities publish learning outcomes in the syllabus. Students rarely read them, and never track against them, so the one artifact that defines what a course expects goes unused all term.

## USER

A university student at the start of a term — or, more often, one picking up a course that is already weeks underway.

## OUTCOME

Every course the student is taking has a named place that knows what the course is trying to teach. Objectives are declared once, up front, so that everything added later can be measured against something. Setup takes a couple of minutes rather than an evening, and starting mid-semester feels normal rather than like arriving late to something already ruined.

## SUCCESS

- A student can go from nothing to a course with objectives in place in under five minutes.
- Most created courses end up with objectives attached rather than left empty. This is the number that matters most: an empty objectives list silently disables readiness, and the student never learns why the product feels thin.
- Students create courses for several subjects, not one. One course means they tried it; several means they adopted it.
- Courses created mid-semester are as likely to stay in use as courses created in week one.

## LIMITS

- Objectives belong to the student and stay editable. Anything read out of a syllabus is a draft they confirm, never a fact imposed on them.
- Pulling objectives out of a document the student already has is acceptable automation — it is transcription, and transcription is not the work that teaches. Deciding what an objective *means* for them is work we must not take away.
- A course must be fully usable with no objectives at all. Readiness stays unavailable until they exist, but capturing, asking and practising must never be blocked behind setup.
- A course belongs to one student. There is no shared, official or verified course record to join.

## NOT NOW

- Importing courses or enrolment from a university system
- A shared catalogue or templates of known courses
- Timetables, class schedules, or anything calendar-shaped
- Any notion of a course being "official", verified, or instructor-owned
