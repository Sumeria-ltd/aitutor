# Intent 0008 — See my readiness

Derived from PRD requirement 0008, rank 8.

PROBLEM:  "Am I ready for this course?" is the question every learner carries all term, and
          not one of them can answer it. What they have instead are proxies: how many lectures
          they attended, whether their folder feels full, how anxious they feel this week. None
          of these correspond to whether they can do what the course demands.
          The strange part is that both halves of the real answer already exist and are never
          put side by side. The course declared what it expects, in objectives nobody reads
          after week one. The learner has been generating evidence about what they can actually
          do all term, in the form of summaries written and practice taken. Nothing brings the
          expectation and the evidence together. So gaps stay invisible until they are graded.
          A learner can work hard all term on the parts they already enjoy and never notice the
          objective they have no material for and have never once tested themselves on.

USER:     A learner partway through a term deciding what to study next — and the same learner
          in the week before an exam, deciding where to spend the little time that remains.

OUTCOME:  The learner sees, objective by objective, what the course expects and what they have
          actually done about it: whether material exists for it, whether they have tested
          themselves on it, and how that went. "Am I behind?" becomes a question with an
          answer. More usefully, "what should I do next?" gets a specific one instead of a
          vague dread. It also makes a term's accumulated effort visible — which is what keeps
          the effort worth making in the weeks when no exam is close enough to supply the
          motivation.

SUCCESS:  Within one week of this existing, readiness is opened on five courses that each have
          at least one objective with no material and no practice behind it. Count the ones
          where that untouched objective is visible without scrolling past what the learner has
          already invested in: five. Count the grade predictions or score estimates anywhere in
          the view: zero.

LIMITS:   Readiness reports evidence, never a predicted grade. It says what the learner has and
          has not done, not how they will score. It must be honest about thin evidence: a gap
          has to look like a gap, even when the learner has been working hard elsewhere.
          Untouched objectives are the most important thing it shows and must not be crowded
          out by the areas the learner has already invested in — surfacing what is being avoided
          is the whole point. It depends on objectives existing; where there are none it must
          say so and point at fixing that, rather than presenting a confident empty state. It is
          a study aid for one learner: not a report to anyone else, and nothing in its design
          should assume a future reader other than them.

NOT NOW:  Sharing readiness with instructors, institutions, or parents. Predicted grades or
          exam-score estimates. Comparison against other learners or cohort averages. Planning
          the learner's week or scheduling their study time.

## Open questions
- How is a piece of material or a practice result connected to an objective? Nothing in 0003,
  0004 or 0007 asks the learner to make that link, and inferring it is the hard part of this
  requirement — possibly the hard part of the product.
- If readiness says a learner is ready and the exam disagrees, this requirement is worse than
  useless: it replaced a vague accurate anxiety with a precise false comfort. What would tell us
  that is happening, given there is no exam result inside the product?
- The stated limit is no predicted grade, but a coverage picture will be read as one regardless.
  How is that misreading designed against?
