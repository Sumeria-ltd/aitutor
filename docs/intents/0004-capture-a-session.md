# Intent 0004 — Capture a session

Derived from PRD requirement 0004, rank 4.

PROBLEM:  The lecture ends. The learner walks out holding two things: a file — emailed,
          downloaded, or photographed off the whiteboard — and a fresh but fragile
          understanding of what just happened. Both decay immediately, and neither decay is
          noticed.
          The file joins two hundred others with names like `Lecture4_final_v2.pdf`. The
          understanding fades over the following days, and nothing ever asks the learner to
          produce it, so they never find out it is going. Nothing in the learner's life marks
          that a class happened, connects the artifact to what it was about, or asks what they
          took away while the answer is still available. The whole term is lost in this gap.
          Learners intend to review, and do not. By the time either the file or the
          understanding is needed, one is unfindable and the other is gone.

USER:     A learner who has just left a class — or is catching up on one they missed — holding
          material and a memory that is still warm.

OUTCOME:  Each class that happened has a marked place in its course, with its material
          attached and, when the learner does the work, their own account of what it covered.
          The file stops being an anonymous download and becomes *session 5 of
          Thermodynamics, the one about entropy*. Capturing takes a minute or two, works from
          a phone in the corridor outside the lecture hall, and does not require the learner
          to have understood everything yet. The invitation to write down what they took away
          arrives here, while the memory is warm — not in exam week, when it is already gone.

SUCCESS:  Within one week of capture existing, one learner captures at least three sessions
          from a phone. Count those completed in under two minutes: all three. Count those
          done on a deliberately poor connection: at least one. Count those carrying the
          learner's own written account rather than only a file: at least one.

LIMITS:   Capture must work when the learner has only a file and nothing yet to say about it.
          Writing their own account is always invited and never required, or capture stops
          happening at all. That account is the learner's work: it may be prompted,
          questioned, or responded to, but must never be written for them and offered back as
          theirs. Sessions can be added late, out of order, or in a rush, without penalty and
          without nagging — learners fall behind, and the product absorbs that rather than
          moralizing about it. Material must be capturable from a phone, immediately after
          class, on a bad connection. Attached material has hard ceilings, and capture must
          fail legibly at them rather than accept something that silently never becomes
          usable. What the learner attaches is theirs, and private to them.

NOT NOW:  Recording or transcribing lecture audio. Reminders, streaks, or any prompting to
          capture. Pulling material automatically from an institution's system. Live
          note-taking during the lecture itself.

## Open questions
- What is a session when nothing schedules one? On a semester course it is a class that
  happened; self-paced, the learner draws the boundary, and a badly drawn one makes 0006 coarse
  and 0008 flat. PRD open question 2.
- Capture must finish in under two minutes on a bad connection, but material has to be read
  before it is useful. What does the learner see in between, and what have they been promised
  once they close the page?
- If material fails to process hours later, how does the learner find out? They have left. An
  in-session message they never return to see is not telling them — but notifications are out
  of scope for v1.
