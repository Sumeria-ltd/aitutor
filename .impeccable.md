# Design context for AITutor

Read by the `frontend-design` and `critique` skills before any design work (see
`.claude/SKILLS/IMPECCABLE.md`). Written by `teach-impeccable` on 2026-09-19 from the PRD,
the product invariants in `CLAUDE.md`, and the human's answers. Product intent lives in the
PRD; this page only says how it should *feel* and *look*. Update it when a decision below
changes — it is the input every screen is judged against.

## Design Context

### Users

A learner on a structured, semester-shaped course — a fixed run of weeks, declared
objectives, a graded event at the end. Beachhead is MENA (Station J, universities).
The v1 interface is English, built RTL-ready: logical inline-start/end everywhere, no
left/right, so an Arabic build mirrors later without redrawing.

They arrive in four situations, and every screen is designed for the one it serves:

| Situation | Where they are | What they want from the screen |
|---|---|---|
| Thirty seconds after class | Corridor, phone, bad connection, one hand | Put the file somewhere and leave — under two minutes, nothing else required |
| Mid-assignment | A specific question; they already own the material | An answer they can *verify*: the citation is the point, one tap from the source |
| The evening after | At a desk, willing to do real work | The work to return something visible, on the same screen, within seconds |
| The week before the exam | Little time left | To see where they are thin, objective by objective, and choose where to spend it |

The job to be done: keep the trail of each class findable, and find out — while there is
still time to act — whether the understanding survived.

Explicitly not for, and the product should feel wrong to them: someone with no course who
wants a document question-answering tool; a study group wanting a shared space; an
instructor wanting to watch a cohort; a learner who wants the answer without doing the work.

### Brand Personality

**Quiet, exacting, on your side.**

A co-learner, not an assistant. It does not greet, perform, congratulate or nudge. It tells
the learner precisely what they got wrong and where it lives, and says plainly when their
material does not cover something — that is a correct answer, not a failure.

Emotional goals: the learner feels the space is *theirs* and *private*; calm on arrival,
clear on leaving. Being shown a gap feels like information, never a telling-off. Four minutes
in and out is the success case — the interface never tries to keep anyone.

Every screen carries two voices: the product's (interface chrome) and the learner's (their
own words). Where the learner's words appear, they are the most important thing on the page.

### Aesthetic Direction

- **Editorial / typographic.** Type does the work: hierarchy comes from scale, weight and
  spacing, not from boxes. A page reads like a well-set book — the learner's words, the
  answers and their citations are the content; the chrome recedes.
- **References.** iA Writer / Bear — one type family, one accent, nothing between you and
  the page. Apple Notes / Craft — familiar, low-ceremony, forgiving.
- **Anti-references.** A chat assistant: no bubbles, no composer pinned to the bottom, no
  persona that "replies". A gamified learning app: no mascots, streaks, confetti, progress
  rings or celebration screens. A file manager: no folders, file icons or filename lists as
  the primary view — material is shown as *sessions*, by what they were about.
- **Theme.** Light only in v1. A dark variant, if it ever comes, is designed as tinted ink
  on warm charcoal, never an inversion with glowing accents.
- **Palette.** Warm neutrals, never cream: a light, barely-warm paper (`#F9F8F3`), warm
  ink (`#241E1A`), every neutral tinted — never pure `#fff` or `#000`. One accent, **board
  green** (`#13543D`, the chalkboard the course was taught on), with a *fixed meaning*: it
  marks what can be pressed and what is a citation, and appears nowhere decorative. There
  is no red and no amber anywhere, so green never means "correct". No purple-to-blue
  gradients, no cyan on dark, no neon, no gradient text.
- **Typography.** **Literata** (a screen-reading serif) for anything that came from the
  learner or their material — titles, objectives, their summary, questions, answers,
  citations. **IBM Plex Sans** for everything the product says. The rule is semantic, not
  decorative: if it is in the serif, it came from the learner. Not Inter, Roboto, Arial or
  system defaults. Arabic companions for the mirrored build: Noto Naskh Arabic and IBM
  Plex Sans Arabic.
- **Layout.** 4px base, fluid spacing that breathes at desk width, phone-width first for
  capture and ask. Hairlines rather than shadows; radii small and consistent. Asymmetry
  where it serves reading; never a card grid.
- **Motion.** State changes only, exponential ease-out, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected.
- **Accessibility.** WCAG 2.2 AA (assumed baseline — not yet confirmed by the human).
  Colour is never the only carrier of meaning; touch targets on capture are 52px or more.
- **The foundation.** `design/foundation/` — `tokens.css` is the record of every exact
  value, `base.css` the primitives (the two voices, the spread, controls, fields, the
  citation, the evidence strip, the states), `index.html` shows them in use. Written
  2026-09-19, v0.1. Every screen is built from it and judged against it.
- **Prior exploration.** A 2026-09-05 mockup set with its own palette and type system exists
  at `git show fe3c550:design/aitutor-ui/` (style sheet: `Styles.dc.html`). Superseded by
  this page on 2026-09-19 — reference for the screen inventory, not authority on the look.

### Design Principles

1. **The learner's words are the loudest thing on the page — and until they have written
   them, nothing stands in.** Their summary, their question and their objectives are set in
   the reading voice and given the most space. Before a summary is written, the screen is
   deliberately empty of their material, and the emptiness is explained so it does not read
   as broken.
2. **Effort pays back on the same screen, within seconds.** Every act of capture, writing or
   practising returns something visible — a check, a coverage change, a result — without
   navigating away. Never a spinner followed by nothing.
3. **A citation is never smaller than the sentence it supports.** Attribution is the
   product's proof and the thing to press: one tap from the source itself. "Your material
   doesn't cover this" is rendered as a first-class answer, with what appears to be
   missing, never as an error.
4. **Gaps are information, never warnings.** Nothing is red because it is unfinished and
   nothing is celebrated because it is done. No scores, streaks, badges, grade predictions
   or nudges anywhere. Untouched objectives are shown first and largest.
5. **Built for the corridor.** Capture works one-handed on a phone on a bad connection:
   large targets, one required thing (the file *or* the words, never both), an honest
   offline state, and nothing lost if the page closes before the work finishes.
